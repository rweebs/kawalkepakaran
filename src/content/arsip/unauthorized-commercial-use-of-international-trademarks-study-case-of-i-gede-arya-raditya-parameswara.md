## Executive Summary

I Gede Arya Raditya Parameswara (hereafter "Defendant") is an Indonesian national currently domiciled in London, United Kingdom, employed as a Software Development Engineer (SDE) at Amazon, specializing in Rust, C++, and Distributed Systems. He is an alumnus of Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB) and holds an active LinkedIn profile with approximately 4,760 followers.

The Defendant operates a paid career mentoring and coaching business at **https://www.gedearya.com/**. On the homepage of this commercial website, the Defendant prominently displays the registered and legally protected logos of **9 (nine) multinational corporations** without authorization from any of the trademark owners. These logos — belonging to companies spanning Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, and the United States — are displayed to create the false impression that these corporations endorse, affiliate with, or approve of the Defendant's coaching services. No such commercial relationship exists.

This constitutes trademark infringement, unfair competition, and fraud under Indonesian law, and trademark infringement under UK law, with criminal exposure under both jurisdictions.

## AEGIS Module F — Critical Defense Analysis

### Defense 1: "Nominative Fair Use"
* **Defendant's Argument:** *"I am only displaying the names of companies where my mentees work."*
* **AEGIS Finding:** **VERY WEAK (2/10)** — Nominative fair use protects text names, not full visual logos (e.g., Gojek owl icon, Traveloka wordmark). Displaying 9 visual logos on a commercial homepage exceeds the necessary minimum.

### Defense 2: "No Consumer Confusion"
* **Defendant's Argument:** *"No reasonable consumer thinks Samsung or J.P. Morgan sponsors my service."*
* **AEGIS Finding:** **WEAK (4/10)** — Under UU Merek Art. 101 and UK TMA Sec. 10(3), well-known marks are protected against **dilution and unfair advantage** without needing to prove consumer confusion.

### Defense 3: "Implied Consent Through Testimonials"
* **Defendant's Argument:** *"My mentees consented to testimonials, extending to company logos."*
* **AEGIS Finding:** **LEGALLY INVALID (1/10)** — Employees have zero legal authority to license corporate registered trademarks.

### Defense 4: "Informational / Non-Commercial Use"
* **Defendant's Argument:** *"My website is informational social proof."*
* **AEGIS Finding:** **LEGALLY INVALID (1/10)** — gedearya.com operates as a commercial conversion funnel directing visitors to paid mentoring arrangements.

### Defense 5: "Freedom of Expression" (UK Context)
* **Defendant's Argument:** *"I have a right to free expression under UK law."*
* **AEGIS Finding:** **WEAK (3/10)** — Freedom of expression covers referential facts, not commercial exploitation of trademark logos for profit (*Boehringer Ingelheim v. Swingward*).
