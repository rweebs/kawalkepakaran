## 1. Executive Summary

This proposal compares the engineering expertise of Rahmat Wibowo and Inaki Anduaga using only the source documents supplied in the folder `source/Rahmat Vs Inaki`:

- `founder_story (1).pdf` for Rahmat Wibowo
- `inaki_anduaga_cv.pdf` for Inaki Anduaga

The central conclusion is straightforward: the two profiles are strong in different engineering theaters.

Inaki Anduaga presents as the stronger profile for mature software-platform leadership at scale: organization-wide engineering standardization, TypeScript adoption, microservice templating, staff-plus technical leadership, and long-horizon platform quality inside established European product companies.

Rahmat Wibowo presents as the stronger profile for cloud infrastructure execution, DevOps transformation, cost optimization, and founder-speed delivery: multi-cloud operations, Terraform-led modernization, compliance-sensitive infrastructure, performance improvement, and rapid company formation around engineering talent mobilization.

There is therefore no serious source-based basis for a simplistic "better engineer" conclusion. The correct conclusion is fit-for-mandate:

- If the mandate is platform architecture, software standards, and engineering process at large organizational scale, Inaki is the stronger fit.
- If the mandate is hands-on cloud execution, DevOps modernization, FinOps, regional startup velocity, and engineering-led company building, Rahmat is the stronger fit.
- If the mandate combines execution speed plus platform maturity, the best proposal is a split-role model rather than a winner-takes-all comparison.

## 2. Objective of This Proposal

The objective of this proposal is to produce a defensible comparative assessment of engineering expertise and then convert that assessment into a practical engagement recommendation.

This document is therefore not only descriptive. It is designed to answer a decision question:

**Which engineer is better suited for which type of technical mission?**

3. Breadth of technical stack

## 7. Bottom-Line Findings

### 7.1 Where Rahmat Clearly Leads

Rahmat leads on:

- Platform standardization with the added worldwide framework reference
- Cloud execution under delivery pressure
- DevOps and infrastructure modernization
- Cost optimization with explicit business impact
- Multi-cloud practical implementation
- High-agency execution in ambiguous or fast-moving environments
- Founder-speed building around engineering capability
- Staff-plus leadership through current CEO responsibility

### 7.2 Where Inaki Clearly Leads

Inaki leads on:

- Principal-level software engineering seniority
- Organization-wide engineering systems thinking
- Microservice-scale architecture
- Platform quality, reuse, and internal developer enablement
- Long-horizon technical stewardship in mature product organizations

### 7.3 Where the Comparison Should Not Be Oversimplified

The source base does not support a simplistic ranking such as "Inaki is stronger overall" or "Rahmat is stronger overall." That framing would collapse two genuinely different kinds of engineering strength into one axis and would be analytically weak.

The stronger statement is this:

- Inaki is more senior in microservice-scale architecture and long-horizon engineering organization design.
- Rahmat is more execution-heavy in cloud infrastructure, DevOps transformation, and founder-led delivery momentum.

## 8. Proposal: How to Use This Comparison in Practice

### 8.1 Option A: Choose Inaki as the Primary Technical Lead

This option is best if the business problem is:

- Too much architectural inconsistency
- Fragmented engineering standards
- Scaling pain across many teams or services
- Need for staff-plus or principal-level platform leadership

Proposal outcome: use Inaki as the primary technical authority for platform direction, engineering standards, reusable tooling, and long-term architecture quality.

### 8.2 Option B: Choose Rahmat as the Primary Technical Lead

This option is best if the business problem is:

- Delivery needs to move fast
- Infrastructure is expensive, unstable, or immature
- The company needs DevOps, Terraform, cloud modernization, or operational discipline
- The environment resembles a startup, turnaround, or execution-heavy transformation

Proposal outcome: use Rahmat as the primary technical lead for execution velocity, infrastructure modernization, cloud governance, and engineering-to-business traction.

### 8.3 Option C: Split-Role Engagement Model

This is the strongest proposal when both speed and maturity matter.

Suggested structure:

- **Inaki Anduaga**: Principal Advisor for platform standards, architecture, and engineering process design
- **Rahmat Wibowo**: Execution Lead for cloud operations, DevOps implementation, delivery acceleration, and organizational mobilization

This pairing is strategically attractive because it separates two problems that companies often confuse:

- building the right engineering system
- building and shipping fast enough under real constraints

## 9. Final Recommendation

If the decision must be reduced to one sentence, it should be this:

**Rahmat Wibowo is the stronger revised source-based profile on total metric count, while Inaki Anduaga remains particularly strong in microservice-scale architecture and long-horizon platform stewardship.**

If the decision must instead be reduced to a single metric-count winner, then Rahmat Wibowo wins the comparison by 9 categories to 2.

Accordingly, the recommended proposal is:

1. Choose Inaki for microservice-scale architecture and long-horizon platform stewardship.
2. Choose Rahmat for infrastructure transformation, execution speed, and engineering-led startup or turnaround environments.
3. Choose both in split roles if the actual objective is to compound platform maturity with execution velocity.
