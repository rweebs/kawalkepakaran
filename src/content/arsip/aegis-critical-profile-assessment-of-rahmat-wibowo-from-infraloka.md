### 2. The Central Methodological Problem: "Correlate mastery with IQ 138.53"

The assessment brief requested linking Rahmat's capabilities with a confirmed childhood IQ of 138.53. Under AEGIS standards, we must point out why statistical correlation is impossible from n = 1:

> *"The subject's demonstrated record early elite-university performance, rapid multi-domain skill acquisition, and dense technical output is consistent with above-average general cognitive ability. The record itself is the evidence; the screening score is, at best, a corroborating footnote."*

**Primary Document (S7) Audit Findings:**

* **"Superior" Band, Not "Genius"**: On the test's scale, the score of 138.53 sits inside the 121–139 "Superior" band. Any framing that claims "genius" reads more into the number than the test itself.

### 6. Module [F] Deep-Dive: Auditing the "1 in 8 Billion" Rarity Model

The InfraLoka profile (pp. 13-14) multiplied eight distinct probabilities (ITB Grad × 7x Certified × AWS Intern × hikikomori recovery, etc.) with a "+500,000,000x correction factor" to estimate a rarity of "1 in 8 billion". Module [F] flags this model on several grounds:

* **Statistical Independence is Violated**: The factors are highly correlated (e.g., getting into ITB increases the chance of securing an AWS internship). Multiplying them assumes statistical independence, which artificially inflates the rarity.
* **Fabricated Correction Factor**: The adjustment factor has no empirical basis and is reverse-engineered to land on a memorable figure.
