*When a company's logo sits on a community event, whoever moderates its chat speaks, in practice, for the company. This article asks what participants can expect from that moderator, using one case as the example.*

This is an opinion piece built on one case. It makes no claim about the character of any individual, and it does not assert that anyone broke a rule.

## Scope and identity note

In my complaint to the Telkom Integrity Line I wrote that I **suspect**, but have not proven, that the account "~Dinda Maharani" that removed me from the event group belongs to Dinda Maharani Aprilia, whose public LinkedIn profile says she is Business and Community Lead at Telkom AI Center. The match rests on the name and the affiliation alone. I have asked Telkom to verify it, and I treat it here as unconfirmed. If it is wrong, none of the role-based points below apply to that person.

The facts of the removal are in [what the screenshots show](/arsip/artikel/dinda-maharani-whatsapp-account-removal-telkom-ai-center-what-the-screenshots-show).

## Why the moderator's role matters

A community lead usually has three jobs that can pull against each other:

- **Keep sessions orderly.** Remove spam, abuse and disruption.
- **Protect the sponsor's reputation.** Avoid public accusations against a partner or speaker during an event.
- **Serve the participants.** Make the event worth attending, which includes trust that the speaker is who the poster says.

When a warning about a speaker arrives mid-event, the first two jobs favour removal and the third favours a reply. Neither choice is wrong in itself. The question is what happens after.

## What participants can reasonably expect

1. **A written moderation rule**, posted at the start: what is allowed, who decides, how to appeal.
2. **A private channel for concerns**, so that a participant has an option other than the live chat.
3. **A named person for verification questions**, with a reply time.
4. **A reply**, even a short one, after a removal: what rule applied and how to ask for review.

I saw none of these in the group, but my view was limited to a screenshot and my own messages, so I cannot say none existed.

## Where I may be wrong

- The account holder may be someone I have misidentified.
- Event staff may have had instructions from others, or a policy I never saw.
- My own message was accusatory, and that gave a moderator a reasonable ground to act.
- A community lead at a large organisation carries many events at once. A slow reply may be a capacity issue, not a choice.
