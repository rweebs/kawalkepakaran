## Why I am writing this

I am a person with a psychosocial disability. I live with bipolar disorder, among other conditions. I am also in a dispute with Rahma Utami, who founded Suarise, an Indonesian social enterprise for digital accessibility and disability inclusion.

Those two facts are why I looked closely at how a founder of a disability-inclusion company speaks in public. I want to be careful about what that does and does not prove, so this post sticks to what the screenshots show, what they do not show, and a fair question: does the practice match the mission?

Per the site screenshots (captured 13 October 2026), Suarise describes itself as *"an Indonesia-based social enterprise focused on enabling digital accessibility and disability inclusion through advocacy, accessibility audit and consultancy, technical capacity building, community engagement, and cross-sector collaboration."* Its home page says *"Business performance and social impact in one leap through digital disability inclusion"* and credits the Kotex #SheCan Fund Indonesia. ## What I am not saying

- **I am not saying Suarise's accessibility work is bad.** Whether an audit is sound or a WCAG recommendation is right stands or falls on its own. A person's conduct does not make their expertise false. Treating it that way would be the mistake I criticised in [Laughter Is Not a Rebuttal](/arsip/artikel/laughter-is-not-a-rebuttal-logic-audit-fuadit-muhammad-plus62-ai-threads).
- **I am not saying every post above was aimed at me.** I have marked where the target is my reading.
- **I am not saying she may not criticise me, or be angry about a legal notice.** She may. Disagreement and anger are not the issue.
- **I am not saying "she is a hypocrite" as a conclusion.** Calling someone a hypocrite does not answer a legal notice, and I do not use it as one.

## The fair question

What I do say is narrower. A founder whose public identity is disability inclusion invites a reasonable expectation from the disabled people she says she serves: that her own public language will not use illness as an insult, and will not mock a person who is, or may be, one of those people. That expectation holds whether or not she knew about me, because words like "sick" and "crazy" used as insults land on everyone who lives with those conditions.

This is where an inclusion company is different from other firms. The mission is not a product line that sits beside the founder's personal conduct. It is a claim about how people with disabilities are treated, so the way its founder talks in public is evidence about whether the claim is lived.

## Five questions I would welcome answers to

1. If "Sakit ni orang" was aimed at me, would she retract or clarify it?
2. Does Suarise have a code of conduct, or language guidance, that covers founders' personal public speech, including the use of illness words as insults?
3. Does Suarise's scope include psychosocial disability, and if so, how?
4. Which specific parts of my notice does she dispute: the facts, the reading of the posts, or the law?
5. Would she be open to a mediated conversation about the posts, separate from the legal notice?
