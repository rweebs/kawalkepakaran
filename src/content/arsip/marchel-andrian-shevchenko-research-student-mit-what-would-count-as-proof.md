*A title like "Research Student" can mean many things. This article asks what documents would settle what it means in one public profile, instead of arguing from screenshots.*

This is a methods piece. It does not conclude that the claim is false, and it does not conclude that it is true.

## Scope and identity note

The profile belongs to Marchel Andrian Shevchenko, speaker at a Telkom AI Center of Excellence event on 8 November 2026. His LinkedIn education section lists "Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Research Student, Computer Science, Sep 2024 – Present", and his Instagram bio reads "Research Student @mit". The national higher-education database entry I captured lists him as an undergraduate at Universitas Teknologi Yogyakarta. These are screenshots from around 29 August 2026 and are not authenticated. See [the side-by-side](/arsip/artikel/telkom-ai-center-indigo-speaker-claims-vs-public-records-what-is-and-is-not-shown).

## Why the two entries are not automatically in conflict

An undergraduate elsewhere can do research with an MIT lab, through a volunteer arrangement, an online programme, a visiting scheme or a collaboration. "Research Student" is a loose phrase, not a degree. So the useful question is not "is he a PhD student" (his own "About" text says he is finishing an undergraduate degree), but "what is the actual relationship, and does the title describe it fairly".

## What would count as proof

Ranked from strongest to weakest:

1. **Written confirmation from MIT** (a department, lab or programme office) stating the nature and dates of the relationship.
2. **A letter from a named supervisor** at MIT, on institutional letterhead, with a way to verify it.
3. **A programme record**, such as acceptance into a named visiting or online research programme.
4. **Research output**: a paper, preprint or repository showing the speaker as an author or contributor alongside MIT-affiliated researchers.
5. **A public acknowledgement** from a lab or faculty member.

A profile, a bio or a third party's repeating of the title are not proof of the relationship.

## What would count against it

Not finding items 1 to 5 does not count against it. Only a written statement from the institution that no such relationship exists would. I have not obtained one and do not claim to.

## What I found

I have not found any item in the list above. That is limited to my own search, which is small, so it is not evidence of absence.

## Where I may be wrong

- The relationship may exist in a form that leaves no public trace.
- I may have missed a paper or acknowledgement.
- The title may be a loose but honest description.
